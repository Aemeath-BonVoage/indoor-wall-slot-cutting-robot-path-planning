# Indoor Wall Slot-Cutting Robot: Fine Alignment, Code-Constrained Slot Planning and Multi-Station Execution

**Abstract of the technical report** (full report in Chinese: [README.md](README.md))

**Problem.** Electrical conduits in residential walls are embedded in slots cut into the wall. A mobile manipulator that cuts them must position its base to millimetre accuracy: the arm reaches only about 12 cm along the wall, and every base pose error is amplified at the tool tip by a 0.69 m lever arm. It must also plan slot routes that satisfy building codes while minimising execution time, and it must be evaluated in a simulator that actually behaves as commanded.

**System.** The robot is simulated in ROS 2 Humble and Gazebo Classic. It has a mecanum omnidirectional base, a decoupled Cartesian 4-DoF arm, a 2D LiDAR and a front camera. The pipeline runs end to end. SLAM Toolbox and Nav2 bring the base to the wall, and fine alignment parks it 0.70 m in front of a fiducial marker. An offline planner outputs slot routes, base stations and arm waypoints. A behaviour tree (BehaviorTree.CPP, structured like the Nav2 `bt_navigator`) then sequences skill actions that visit the stations, hold the base, compensate residual pose errors and cut. Nav2 uses Smac 2D planning and an omnidirectional MPPI controller. The base runs under ros2_control with a custom mecanum controller and a simulated hardware interface. Its wheel odometry includes an encoder error model and is fused with a simulated gyro by a `robot_localization` EKF. Cutting is simulated with a 10 mm tool-to-wall gap, since Gazebo cannot model material removal. Gazebo ground truth is used only for evaluation.

**Results.** Numbers are Gazebo measurements unless marked *offline*.

- **Fine alignment.** Detection, estimation, control and the arrival test are separated, following Nav2 `opennav_docking`. The detector uses AprilTag with edge-fitted sub-pixel corners and IPPE. A wall-relative EKF over (distance, lateral offset, heading) uses exact SE(2) odometry increments, latency compensation and χ² gating. The control law is a square-root-shaped saturated proportional law, globally asymptotically stable for the integrator plant (V = ½|e|²). A discrete-time pole analysis with sampling, velocity-loop lag and one-step delay shows about 3× gain margin at the actual 100 ms period. In paired trials (n = 8 per condition), the arrival error falls from 4.16 ± 0.94 mm to 0.53 ± 0.21 mm. It stays at 0.5–0.8 mm under odometry drift, cool lighting and intermittent occlusion. Under full occlusion the new pipeline refuses to move, whereas the colour-blob baseline converges with a silent 8.6 mm bias. Taught zero-offset calibration reduces the effect of a 1° camera mounting error from 10.6 mm to 1.04 mm. There were no collisions in 184 trials.
- **Code-constrained slot planning.** A C++ A\* treats whole straight segments as edges. This lets it enforce building-code rules as hard constraints: no horizontal slots in load-bearing walls, limited horizontal runs, and the conduit bending radius. Optimality is kept with the state (x, y, last direction), which was checked against an independent Dijkstra on 400 random grids. On 140 random walls (*offline*) the planner outputs no violating route, whereas the first-version obstacle-only planner violates the code on 106 walls.
- **Task-level joint planning.** Station partitioning uses exact dynamic programming, and the visiting order is a closed-form one-dimensional sweep. Station-aware route search moves slot corners onto station-window boundaries. A lower-bound certificate tests optimality on every wall. On 400 random residential walls (*offline*, model-predicted time), joint planning is never slower than plan-then-partition. It saves a further 3.5 % on non-load-bearing walls (95 % CI [3.1, 4.0] %, Wilcoxon p < 1e-10) and is proven optimal on 92 % of walls. The saving comes from fewer arm waypoints. The room3 example task was then executed in Gazebo, paired by slip seed (2 + 2 runs). The joint plan took 228 s against 246 s for the rule-based routes, a measured saving of 7.3 % against a predicted 6.6 %, with the same arrival and tool-path accuracy.
- **Multi-station execution.** With ground-truth odometry, two slots (2.76 m, 11 stations) were completed in every end-to-end run. The optimised partition and order reduced station time from 283 s to 250 s (−11.8 %). A constant per-run lateral offset of +0.65 to +2.29 mm was inherited from the alignment parking residual. Using the EKF-estimated marker as the wall origin reduced it to +0.24 / −0.18 mm (an ablation gave +0.86 mm), with a maximum tool-path error of 0.93 mm. The execution-time model is recalibrated whenever the execution path changes, and each calibration is kept as a named preset. On the current default path, which returns the arm to an observation pose before each strafe, the previous preset underestimated every run by 18–21 s. A phase-wise fit with a ratio estimate for the cutting phase spread a fixed 1.0 s per-station arm move over the waypoints and overstated the per-waypoint time by 6 %. A regression for that phase fixes this. The resulting preset `align_v3` has a per-station RMSE of 0.22 s on 44 stations, and on 5 runs not used for calibration its error in total station time stays within 0.7 %.
- **System layer.** Behaviour-tree execution matched the earlier sequential program on the 11-station plan: maximum tool-path error 1.36 mm and 232.5 s versus 233.2–233.5 s. Over 3 laps of 6 fixed goals, Smac 2D + MPPI reached 18 / 18 goals versus 15 / 18 for NavFn + DWB. It kept 183 mm minimum clearance versus 5 mm and used 9.7 % versus 45.5 % of one core in the controller. Fusing the gyro reduced odometry drift over a 129 m route from 1.4–1.6 % to 0.14–0.26 %. Two findings shaped the design. Part of the base rotation in Gazebo appears only in the pose and not in link velocities, so the velocity loop and sensors now use pose-differenced velocity. The EKF output under-reports mm/s-level motion, so local loops such as base holding use raw wheel odometry.
- **Realistic odometry and along-wall accuracy.** With the encoder error model and EKF, single-station alignment (0.7 mm) and base holding (0.10 mm drift) were unaffected. With a single marker and odometry-only strafing, however, the along-wall error grew linearly with distance from the marker, up to 24.7 mm. A planar wall gives the LiDAR no along-wall information, so the position between stations is extrapolated from odometry, and the error is a fixed-direction lateral scale error (the 3 % lateral-slip term). Three remedies were compared in Gazebo (2 runs per configuration). A map of AprilTags with known along-wall positions works when the spacing stays within the camera's visible window of about 0.83 m: 2.8 mm at 0.6 m spacing, but 11.9 mm at 1.2 m. Re-aligning at the reference marker after a set travel does not help against a fixed-direction scale error (17.5 mm, +48 s). Estimating the lateral odometry scale online as a fourth EKF state converges to −2.7 to −3.0 % (σ ≈ 0.3 %), matching the slip model. The default now combines a 0.6 m tag map with scale estimation: the maximum along-wall error falls from 24.7 mm to 1.5 mm (mean 0.7 mm), tool-path p95 error is 0.23 mm, and total time rises from 272 s to 295 s. Errors in the recorded tag positions pass into the result almost one to one (±3 mm gives 5.2 mm).
- **Simulation fidelity.** The base turned at only 18–37 % of the commanded rate. Two interacting causes were found: position-interface arm joints and a planar-move plugin that assigns one velocity to all links. Force-controlled joints and a custom force-based base plugin restore the ratio to 0.99–1.01.
- **Method comparison.** About 12,500 offline trials show that pure IBVS with a 5 cm marker is nearly unobservable in yaw for this configuration (Jacobian condition number 314). Its apparent robustness to calibration comes from the taught reference, which a position-based scheme can use equally well.

**Limitations.** All results are from simulation, with idealised rendering, an assumed (not calibrated) encoder error model, a force-driven base whose wheels do not carry the load, and no cutting forces. The alignment and most multi-station results were obtained with ground-truth odometry. The along-wall accuracy under realistic odometry relies on a wall-mounted tag map with measured tag positions and assumes a constant lateral slip; marker-free references (corners, door frames, BIM registration) were only analysed. Samples are small: 8 trials per alignment condition, 2 + 1 runs for the final multi-station setup, 1–3 runs per system-layer configuration and 2 runs per along-wall configuration. Task-level gains over the 400 walls are model predictions; only one wall and one task were executed in Gazebo, with 2 runs per plan.
