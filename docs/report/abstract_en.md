# Indoor Wall Slot-Cutting Robot: Fine Alignment, Code-Constrained Slot Planning and Multi-Station Execution

**Abstract of the technical report** (full report in Chinese: [README.md](README.md))

**Problem.** Electrical conduits in residential walls are embedded in slots cut into the wall. A mobile manipulator that cuts them must position its base to millimetre accuracy: the arm reaches only about 12 cm along the wall, and every base pose error is amplified at the tool tip by a 0.69 m lever arm. It must also plan slot routes that satisfy building codes while minimising execution time, and it must be evaluated in a simulator that actually behaves as commanded.

**System.** The robot is simulated in ROS 2 Humble and Gazebo Classic. It has a mecanum omnidirectional base, a decoupled Cartesian 4-DoF arm, a 2D LiDAR and a front camera. The pipeline runs end to end. SLAM Toolbox and Nav2 bring the base to the wall, and fine alignment parks it 0.70 m in front of a fiducial marker. An offline planner outputs slot routes, base stations and arm waypoints. A single executor then visits the stations, holds the base, compensates residual pose errors and cuts. Cutting is simulated with a 10 mm tool-to-wall gap, since Gazebo cannot model material removal. Gazebo ground truth is used only for evaluation.

**Results.** Numbers are Gazebo measurements unless marked *offline*.

- **Fine alignment.** Detection, estimation, control and the arrival test are separated, following Nav2 `opennav_docking`. The detector uses AprilTag with edge-fitted sub-pixel corners and IPPE. A wall-relative EKF over (distance, lateral offset, heading) uses exact SE(2) odometry increments, latency compensation and χ² gating. The control law is a square-root-shaped saturated proportional law, globally asymptotically stable for the integrator plant (V = ½|e|²). A discrete-time pole analysis with sampling, velocity-loop lag and one-step delay shows about 3× gain margin at the actual 100 ms period. In paired trials (n = 8 per condition), the arrival error falls from 4.16 ± 0.94 mm to 0.53 ± 0.21 mm. It stays at 0.5–0.8 mm under odometry drift, cool lighting and intermittent occlusion. Under full occlusion the new pipeline refuses to move, whereas the colour-blob baseline converges with a silent 8.6 mm bias. Taught zero-offset calibration reduces the effect of a 1° camera mounting error from 10.6 mm to 1.04 mm. There were no collisions in 184 trials.
- **Code-constrained slot planning.** A C++ A\* treats whole straight segments as edges. This lets it enforce building-code rules as hard constraints: no horizontal slots in load-bearing walls, limited horizontal runs, and the conduit bending radius. Optimality is kept with the state (x, y, last direction), which was checked against an independent Dijkstra on 400 random grids. On 140 random walls (*offline*) the planner outputs no violating route, whereas the first-version obstacle-only planner violates the code on 106 walls.
- **Task-level joint planning.** Station partitioning uses exact dynamic programming, and the visiting order is a closed-form one-dimensional sweep. Station-aware route search moves slot corners onto station-window boundaries. A lower-bound certificate tests optimality on every wall. On 400 random residential walls (*offline*, model-predicted time), joint planning is never slower than plan-then-partition. It saves a further 3.5 % on non-load-bearing walls (95 % CI [3.1, 4.0] %, Wilcoxon p < 1e-10) and is proven optimal on 92 % of walls. The saving comes from fewer arm waypoints.
- **Multi-station execution.** Two slots (2.76 m, 11 stations) were completed in every end-to-end run. The optimised partition and order reduced station time from 283 s to 250 s (−11.8 %). A constant per-run lateral offset of +0.65 to +2.29 mm was inherited from the alignment parking residual. Using the EKF-estimated marker as the wall origin reduced it to +0.24 / −0.18 mm (an ablation gave +0.86 mm), with a maximum tool-path error of 0.93 mm. The execution-time model was recalibrated for this execution path by a phase-wise fit. It has a per-station RMSE of 0.68 s on 33 stations, versus 1.65 s for the earlier model, which overestimated each station by 1.5 s. Both calibrations are kept as named presets.
- **Simulation fidelity.** The base turned at only 18–37 % of the commanded rate. Two interacting causes were found: position-interface arm joints and a planar-move plugin that assigns one velocity to all links. Force-controlled joints and a custom force-based base plugin restore the ratio to 0.99–1.01.
- **Method comparison.** About 12,500 offline trials show that pure IBVS with a 5 cm marker is nearly unobservable in yaw for this configuration (Jacobian condition number 314). Its apparent robustness to calibration comes from the taught reference, which a position-based scheme can use equally well.

**Limitations.** All results are from simulation, with idealised rendering and odometry, zero wheel friction and no cutting forces. Samples are small: 8 trials per alignment condition and 2 + 1 runs for the final multi-station setup. Task-level gains are model predictions not yet executed in Gazebo. System-layer work is in progress: BehaviorTree orchestration, Nav2 MPPI / Smac Planner, and `robot_localization`.
